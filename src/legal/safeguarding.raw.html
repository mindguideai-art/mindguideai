1. Purpose

MindGuide AI is committed to preventing and reducing the risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect and other avoidable harm connected with its services, people, technology and partnerships. This policy sets minimum standards for protecting users and others who interact with MindGuide AI, and explains how concerns are identified, reported, assessed, recorded and escalated. Safeguarding is everyone’s responsibility. This policy does not replace clinical judgement, professional codes, emergency services, statutory duties or a qualified practitioner’s duty of care.

2. Scope

Applies to all directors, employees, interns, contractors, volunteers, counsellors, therapists, clinicians, moderators, technology providers and partners, as relevant to their role. It covers the website, app, assessments, AI-supported features, communities, messaging, virtual consultations, events, outreach and physical services delivered with partners. Where a partner has a stronger applicable safeguarding standard, follow the stricter standard where lawful.

3. Safeguarding principles

  • Do no harm: identify foreseeable risks and avoid exposing people to further harm.

  • Dignity and respect: treat people fairly, without stigma, discrimination, intimidation or humiliation.

  • Person-centred support: listen, take concerns seriously and involve the person in decisions where safe and appropriate.

  • Prevention: build safeguards into recruitment, service design, technology and partnerships.

  • Proportionality: take and share information only where necessary, lawful and proportionate.

  • Confidentiality with limits: protect information while explaining when disclosure may be necessary to address serious risk or comply with law.

  • Accountability: document decisions, assign responsibility and learn from incidents.

  • Accessibility and inclusion: make reporting and support accessible to people with differing needs.

  • Professional competence: clinical services must be delivered only by appropriately qualified and authorised professionals within their scope.

6. Protection of adults at risk and minors

6.1 Adults at risk

  • Presume adults can make their own decisions unless there is a lawful basis to determine otherwise.

  • Respect autonomy, informed choice and the right to decline support, while responding to serious and immediate risks.

  • Offer accessible communication and reasonable adjustments where possible.

  • Do not assume a diagnosis, disability or distress automatically removes decision-making capacity.

  • Consult the Safeguarding Lead and appropriate professional where abuse, coercion or serious risk is suspected.

6.2 Children and young people under 18

  • Do not launch direct counselling or open community access for minors until child-specific safeguarding, age-appropriate consent, guardian involvement, professional suitability and referral arrangements have been approved.

  • If a minor contacts the service or discloses harm, take the concern seriously and escalate immediately; do not dismiss or investigate it yourself.

  • Use age-appropriate explanations, verified professional suitability and safe communication channels if a child service is approved.

  • Never privately message a child from personal accounts or move a conversation to unapproved channels.

  • The child’s best interests and safety must guide decisions, taking their views into account in an age-appropriate way.

  • Obtain Nigerian legal advice on parental consent, confidentiality, mandatory reporting and child protection before serving minors.

8. Professional boundaries and conduct

  • Treat users respectfully; do not exploit trust, distress, dependency or a professional relationship.

  • Do not pursue romantic, sexual, financial or exploitative relationships with users.

  • Do not request loans, gifts, favours, passwords, intimate images or personal benefits.

  • Use approved communication channels; do not contact users from personal accounts unless expressly authorised under a documented procedure.

  • Do not diagnose, prescribe or recommend medication changes unless qualified and authorised.

  • Do not record sessions, take screenshots or use user stories for marketing, training or research without a lawful basis and specific informed consent.

  • Declare conflicts of interest and report boundary breaches promptly.

9. Reporting safeguarding concerns

  1. Listen calmly, take the person seriously and avoid blame or leading questions.

  2. Check whether anyone is in immediate danger and whether urgent medical or emergency assistance is needed.

  3. Explain that privacy will be respected but absolute secrecy cannot be promised where sharing is necessary to protect someone or comply with law.

  4. Record the concern promptly, using the person’s own words where possible; separate facts, quotations and observations from assumptions.

  5. Report through the approved secure channel to the Safeguarding Lead. If that person is implicated or unavailable, contact the deputy or CEO/designated alternative.

  6. Do not investigate, confront the alleged perpetrator, interview witnesses or promise an outcome.

  7. Preserve relevant records securely and share only with people who need to know.

10. Responding to distress or crisis

MindGuide AI is not an emergency response service. AI outputs, automated flags and community moderators must not be presented as substitutes for emergency care, clinical assessment or crisis intervention.

  • Take immediate danger, suicidal intent, self-harm, violence, abuse or medical emergency seriously and escalate promptly to a qualified human professional or emergency service.

  • Where safe and appropriate, encourage the user to contact local emergency services, attend the nearest emergency department or contact a trusted person who can be physically present.

  • A trained clinician should assess clinical risk; non-clinical staff must not attempt an assessment beyond their training.

  • For virtual sessions, clinicians should follow an agreed emergency protocol, including location and emergency contact steps where lawful, necessary and appropriately explained.

  • Do not leave a person at imminent risk without taking reasonable steps to connect them with urgent support.

  • Document the concern, actions, advice, referrals and rationale; arrange a post-incident review where appropriate.

11. Referral and escalation

Operational level Examples Response
Immediate / imminent Current attempt or imminent intent with means; serious injury; immediate violence; child in immediate danger. Contact emergency / urgent clinical services; alert Safeguarding Lead and clinician; share minimum necessary information; document.
High / urgent Recent self-harm; escalating suicidal thoughts; suspected exploitation; serious deterioration. Prompt same-day review by qualified clinician or safeguarding professional; agree safety and referral plan.
Moderate Significant distress without immediate danger; repeated concerning disclosures; suspected neglect without immediate threat. Timely professional review, suitable referral and follow-up; record and monitor.
Routine General emotional difficulty without identified safeguarding indicators. Provide information, routine referral or service pathway; document as appropriate.

These levels are operational prompts, not diagnostic categories. Escalate uncertainty to the Safeguarding Lead or qualified clinician. Maintain a verified directory of hospitals, licensed professionals, child protection services and emergency contacts in every service area.

12. Confidentiality and information sharing

  • Collect only information necessary for a defined purpose and explain that purpose.

  • Restrict sensitive information to authorised personnel with a genuine need to know.

  • Use approved systems; do not store user records on personal devices or personal cloud accounts.

  • Explain confidentiality limits before assessment or counselling begins.

  • Information may be shared without consent only where a valid legal basis exists, such as serious and imminent risk, a child protection concern, a lawful order or another applicable legal obligation.

  • Share the minimum necessary with an appropriate recipient; document the rationale, recipient, date and information shared.

  • Do not promise absolute anonymity or confidentiality.

13. Complaints and incident management

  • Provide accessible complaint routes, including a route outside the person complained about.

  • Acknowledge complaints promptly, assess immediate safety and explain next steps.

  • Assign an impartial reviewer; consider temporary contact or access restrictions where necessary to protect people and preserve a fair process.

  • Maintain a secure incident register recording date, category, risk, actions, referrals, decisions, owner and outcome.

  • Protect people who raise concerns in good faith from retaliation.

  • Preserve evidence, cooperate with relevant authorities where required and implement corrective actions after review.

14. Safer recruitment and partner due diligence

  • Define safeguarding requirements for each role before recruitment or engagement.

  • Verify identity, qualifications, professional registration and references for people providing clinical or counselling services.

  • Conduct lawful and proportionate background checks where available and appropriate, especially for child-facing roles.

  • Assess partners’ policies, complaints processes, supervision, data protection and incident reporting.

  • Use written agreements requiring safeguarding compliance, prompt reporting, confidentiality and secure data handling.

  • Do not describe a person as a licensed clinician until credentials and registration have been verified.

15. Training and compliance

  • Complete safeguarding induction before user-facing work or access to sensitive records.

  • Provide role-specific training for moderators, counsellors, clinicians, managers and technical personnel.

  • Refresh training at least annually and after material changes or relevant incidents.

  • Maintain a training register and signed acknowledgement of the policy.

  • Monitor compliance through access reviews, partner reviews, incident analysis, user feedback and periodic audits.

  • Breaches may lead to retraining, access restriction, suspension, termination or referral, as appropriate and lawful.

16. Record keeping and review

Safeguarding records must be accurate, factual, access-controlled and retained only for an approved period consistent with legal, regulatory, clinical and operational requirements. Establish a retention schedule, secure deletion process, breach response process and documented access permissions. Review this policy at least annually, after serious incidents, when services or target users change, or when law or professional guidance changes.
