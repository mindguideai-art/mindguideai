1. Purpose and outcomes

This framework explains how MindGuide AI will protect user identity and sensitive mental health information while enabling assessments, resources, communities, therapist matching, appointments and virtual consultations.

  • Give users meaningful choice about what identity information they disclose and where it appears.

  • Minimise collection and exposure of personal and health information.

  • Separate public-facing identity from verified identity where the service requires both.

  • Limit staff and partner access to information needed for their duties.

  • Explain confidentiality limits and crisis escalation before users share sensitive information.

  • Balance privacy with proportionate action where serious harm is suspected.

2. Design principles

  • Privacy by design and default: build privacy controls into the product.

  • Data minimisation: collect only what is necessary for a specified purpose.

  • Purpose limitation: do not reuse sensitive data for unrelated marketing, profiling or research without a valid basis and appropriate notice/consent.

  • Least privilege: staff and partners see only what their role requires.

  • Transparency: explain what is collected, why, who can see it, retention and disclosure circumstances.

  • Security: protect data in transit and at rest; use authentication, logging, backups and secure deletion.

  • User control: provide ways to review, correct or request deletion where legally available.

  • Safety: do not let anonymity create false promises or prevent lawful safeguarding action.

3. Public display name and verified identity

Service area Recommended approach
Community spaces Allow a pseudonymous display name. Do not show legal name, phone, email, address, appointment history or assessment results.
Account registration Collect only details needed for account security and service delivery; explain any email/phone verification.
Matching and booking Collect minimum identity/contact details needed to arrange care and support safe service delivery.
Virtual counselling Practitioner may need to verify identity and current location for clinical safety; explain this before the session.
Payments Use a reputable payment provider; avoid storing full card details on MindGuide AI systems unless authorised and compliant.
Safeguarding / emergency Restrict access to identifying details to designated personnel and use only where necessary, lawful and proportionate.

Do not describe a service as fully anonymous if it collects contact details, device identifiers, payment information or appointment data that can identify or link to a person. Use accurate terms such as 'pseudonymous community display' or 'confidential account'.

4. Information classification

Class Examples Minimum standard
Public Approved articles and public event details. Approved for public release; check for personal data.
Internal Non-sensitive operational documents and team schedules. Staff access only; approved storage.
Confidential personal Name, email, phone, account identifiers and appointments. Role-based access; secure transfer/storage; no public display.
Restricted sensitive Assessments, counselling notes, diagnosis, crisis and safeguarding records. Strict need-to-know access; enhanced authentication; audit logs; defined retention; no unapproved tools.

5. Consent and transparency

  • Provide a clear privacy notice before or when personal information is collected.

  • Explain the purpose of each sensitive-data collection, whether it is optional and consequences of not providing it.

  • Obtain valid, appropriately recorded consent or another lawful basis where required; do not bundle unrelated purposes into blanket consent.

  • Use separate optional consent for marketing, testimonials, research, recordings or secondary use of user stories.

  • Explain who may access information, including clinicians, service providers and authorised staff.

  • Explain confidentiality limits, safeguarding disclosures and emergency escalation in plain language.

  • Establish age-appropriate consent and guardian processes before making any service available to minors; do not launch minor services until legally reviewed and operationally ready.

6. Community anonymity and moderation

  • Allow a display name distinct from the legal name, subject to rules against impersonation and abuse.

  • Do not display legal names, contact details, precise location, therapist notes or assessment results in public profiles.

  • Give users controls for profile visibility and whether others may find or contact them.

  • Prohibit doxxing, threats, harassment, sexual solicitation, coercion, discriminatory abuse and sharing another person’s private information.

  • Provide report, block and moderation functions.

  • Train moderators to recognise safeguarding disclosures and escalate rather than attempt clinical intervention.

  • Explain that posts are not completely anonymous: other users may copy, screenshot or identify content from context.

  • Explain that community spaces are not emergency services or a substitute for professional care.

7. Access controls

Role Minimum permitted access
Community moderator Community content and moderation tools; no routine clinical-note access.
Customer support Account/service information needed to resolve support requests; no routine counselling-note access.
Counsellor / clinician Records of assigned users and information necessary for care.
Safeguarding Lead Safeguarding records and necessary user details for risk response; access logged.
Technology administrator Technical access only when necessary; avoid routine content viewing; privileged actions logged.
Management Aggregated operational reporting by default; identifiable sensitive records only when necessary and authorised.

  • Use individual staff accounts; prohibit shared logins.

  • Apply role-based permissions, least privilege and multi-factor authentication where available.

  • Review access when roles change or personnel leave.

  • Log access to restricted records and review unusual or excessive access.

  • Include confidentiality and data-processing clauses in staff, clinician and vendor agreements.

8. Secure counselling and appointment processes

  • Use approved video, audio and messaging services with appropriate security and contractual safeguards.

  • Do not record sessions by default. If recording is proposed, obtain specific informed consent, define purpose, retention and access, and allow refusal where feasible.

  • Before a session, explain identity verification, confidentiality limits, emergency procedures and technical limitations.

  • For virtual clinical appointments, establish a proportionate process for confirming location and emergency contact arrangements where necessary and lawfully collected.

  • Send discreet appointment reminders; avoid sensitive labels in notifications where possible.

  • Limit appointment access to assigned practitioners and authorised scheduling personnel.

  • Have a secure missed-appointment procedure where a clinician has credible concern about immediate risk.

9. Crisis response and safeguarding disclosures

Do not imply the platform is continuously monitored or that AI provides emergency protection unless this is genuinely staffed, tested and available.

  • Display clear crisis information at relevant points, including onboarding, assessments, communities and counselling.

  • Route indications of immediate danger to a qualified human responder or emergency pathway under the approved protocol.

  • Use AI to support signposting or flag potential risk only with human oversight; do not use automated systems as the sole high-risk decision-maker.

  • Where identifying or location information is needed for urgent response, access and share only the minimum necessary with appropriate responders on a valid legal basis.

  • Explain before use that confidentiality may be limited for serious risk, child protection or legal obligations.

  • Document indicators, human review, rationale, information disclosed and follow-up.

10. Retention, deletion and user requests

  • Create a retention schedule by data category, taking account of legal, clinical, safeguarding and contractual requirements.

  • Do not retain sensitive information indefinitely without a defined reason.

  • Provide a process for access, correction or deletion requests where legally available.

  • Explain that some information may need to be retained for legal claims, safeguarding, professional recordkeeping or another lawful reason.

  • Use secure deletion and propagate deletion to relevant systems/processors where required.

  • Record retention exceptions and their rationale.

11. Third-party processors and partners

  • Conduct privacy/security due diligence before sharing information with hospitals, counsellors, technology providers, cloud hosts or payment providers.

  • Use written agreements defining purpose, permitted data, confidentiality, security, breach notification, retention/deletion, subcontracting and assistance with user rights.

  • Share identifiable information only when necessary for the agreed service and supported by a valid basis and appropriate notice/consent.

  • Do not provide partners unrestricted access to the user database.

  • Review partner access periodically and remove it when no longer required.

12. Data breach and privacy incident response

  1. Anyone suspecting loss, unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration or destruction of personal information must report it immediately through the approved security/privacy channel.

  2. Contain the incident, preserve evidence and restrict compromised access without destroying relevant records.

  3. Assess the information involved, affected people, likely consequences and any safeguarding risk.

  4. Consult the privacy lead and legal counsel to determine notification obligations and timelines under applicable law.

  5. Notify affected people or regulators where legally required, using clear and useful information.

  6. Record the incident, decisions, notifications, remediation and lessons learned.

13. Product and technology requirements

Control Minimum requirement
Account identity Separate public display name from private identifiers; verify only what is necessary.
Authentication Strong password controls, secure recovery and MFA for staff/privileged users.
Authorisation Role-based permissions; separate community, support and clinical records.
Encryption Industry-standard encryption in transit and at rest, managed keys and secure secrets.
Auditability Log access to restricted records, administrative changes, exports and disclosure events.
Notifications Discreet notification content and user-configurable privacy settings.
AI systems Do not send identifiable sensitive data to public or unapproved AI services; document vendors, purpose, safeguards and human oversight.
Backups / deletion Encrypt backups, restrict access, test restoration and define deletion through backup cycles.
Analytics Prefer aggregated or de-identified analytics; suppress small groups where re-identification is possible.

14. Suggested plain-language user wording

Draft wording for adaptation in the privacy notice and onboarding flow; it is not a substitute for a full privacy notice:

You can use a display name in MindGuide AI community spaces. Your display name is separate from the private account information we may need to provide services. We limit access to sensitive mental health information to authorised people who need it for their role. We do not promise complete anonymity: information may be shared where necessary and lawful to respond to a serious safety concern, protect a child or comply with a legal obligation. We aim to share only the information needed and explain our actions where it is safe and lawful. MindGuide AI is not an emergency service. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or attend the nearest emergency department.

15. Governance and testing

  • Assign a named owner for this framework and a privacy/security contact.

  • Complete privacy and safeguarding risk assessments before launching assessments, AI matching, communities and virtual counselling.

  • Test consent, access permissions, crisis escalation, account recovery, deletion and breach response before launch.

  • Review third-party integrations and data flows before enabling them.

  • Train staff, moderators and clinicians and record completion.

  • Review after incidents, material service changes, new user groups or changes in law.
