1. Purpose and status

This policy establishes minimum governance and operating standards for MindGuide AI (the “Company”). It applies to founders, directors, employees, contractors, consultants, interns, volunteers, moderators, developers and approved service partners acting for the Company.

It does not establish that MindGuide AI is incorporated, licensed as a healthcare provider, accredited, or partnered with any hospital, clinician or organisation.

2. Our purpose and approach

MindGuide AI is a technology-enabled mental health and wellbeing platform intended to help people access relevant information, AI-assisted wellbeing guidance, qualified professionals, supportive communities, programmes and referral pathways.

Our approach is human-centred, inclusive, evidence-informed and safety-conscious. Technology may support navigation and personalisation; it does not replace professional clinical judgement, diagnosis, treatment or emergency services.

3. Core principles

  • Respect and dignity: treat every person fairly, without stigma, discrimination, harassment or degrading treatment.

  • Do no harm: identify foreseeable risks, avoid unsupported claims and do not offer services beyond competence or operational capacity.

  • Informed choice: explain services, material risks, data use, fees and alternatives in clear language.

  • Privacy and confidentiality: collect only necessary information, restrict access and disclose information only on a valid basis.

  • Professional accountability: verify qualifications and scope of practice before presenting anyone as a clinician or counsellor.

  • Accessibility and inclusion: make services understandable and usable by people with differing needs, subject to safe delivery.

  • Transparency: distinguish AI-generated information, peer support, general education and professional clinical services.

4. Governance and accountability

The Founder/Chief Executive or formally appointed management body is accountable for approving policies, assigning owners, providing resources and reviewing material risks.

Before services go live, management must appoint in writing a Safeguarding Lead and deputy, a privacy/data-protection lead or qualified Data Protection Officer where required, a clinical governance lead where clinical services are offered, and a complaints/incident contact.

Each service must have a documented owner, approved scope, eligibility criteria, operating hours, escalation route, recordkeeping rules and service limitations. Personnel must not promise an unapproved service, partnership, clinical outcome or emergency response.

5. Professional and clinical service standards

Only appropriately qualified, registered and suitably experienced professionals may provide services that legally or professionally require those qualifications or registration. Identity, credentials, registration status, scope of practice, references and relevant restrictions must be checked and periodically re-verified.

Clinicians and counsellors must work within competence, maintain professional boundaries, obtain informed consent, keep appropriate records, explain confidentiality limits and follow applicable professional standards and law.

Peer supporters, volunteers and moderators must not diagnose, prescribe, recommend medication changes or represent themselves as clinicians unless independently qualified and authorised.

Any free or discounted counselling arrangement must be checked and documented for qualifications, capacity, availability, supervision, confidentiality, safeguarding, referral criteria, duration, liability and continuity before referrals begin.

6. AI use and limitations

AI features may support navigation, wellbeing reflection, non-diagnostic check-ins or identification of potentially relevant support options. The interface must explain when a user is interacting with AI.

Unless a feature has been appropriately validated, approved and lawfully delivered, AI output must not be presented as a diagnosis, clinical assessment, treatment plan, prescription or substitute for a qualified professional.

AI recommendations must be reviewed for foreseeable bias, unsafe advice, misleading certainty and inappropriate crisis handling. Clinically consequential decisions must not be made solely by automated systems without safeguards and meaningful human review where required.

The Company must not claim that AI continuously monitors users or that a person will intervene immediately unless such a service is genuinely staffed, tested and operational.

7. Safeguarding and crisis response

The Company must maintain safeguarding procedures for children and adults at risk, including safer recruitment, staff training, reporting, recordkeeping, referral and escalation.

No service should be made available to minors until age limits, age-appropriate information, consent and guardian procedures, professional suitability, moderation and child-specific safeguards have been reviewed and approved.

If a user may be in immediate danger, personnel must follow the approved crisis protocol and encourage the person to contact local emergency services or attend the nearest hospital emergency department. The Platform must not be represented as an emergency response service.

Disclosures of abuse, exploitation, neglect, self-harm or threats of harm must be handled respectfully and promptly. Staff must not investigate allegations themselves or promise absolute secrecy. Information may be shared only where necessary and lawful to protect a person or comply with legal obligations.

8. Privacy, confidentiality and security

Personal information, particularly mental-health and other sensitive information, must be processed lawfully, fairly, transparently and for specified legitimate purposes. Apply data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, access controls and appropriate security safeguards.

Access to user records must be role-based and limited to authorised personnel who need the information. Staff must use approved systems, strong authentication and secure devices; personal accounts and unauthorised channels must not be used to store or discuss identifiable user information.

Before sensitive data is collected, identify and document an applicable lawful basis and satisfy any additional conditions under Nigerian law. Consent must be specific, informed and freely given where consent is relied upon.

Third-party processors must be assessed and bound by written terms covering confidentiality, security, permitted processing, incident notification, deletion/return and assistance with data-subject rights. International transfers must be assessed under applicable law.

Suspected loss, unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration or destruction of personal data must be reported immediately. The privacy lead must assess, contain, document and make legally required notifications within applicable timelines.

9. User anonymity and community safety

Where available, users may use a pseudonymous display name in community spaces. Pseudonymity does not mean complete anonymity: account, contact, payment, device or technical information may still identify or be linked to a user.

Community spaces must have published rules, trained moderation, reporting/blocking tools and procedures for harassment, threats, exploitation, misinformation and disclosure of private information.

Users should not be encouraged to share identifying or highly sensitive information publicly. Moderators must not disclose a user's identity or participation except where authorised and lawful.

10. Staff conduct

Personnel must act honestly, respectfully and professionally; disclose conflicts of interest; protect Company property and information; comply with lawful instructions; and complete required safeguarding, privacy and security training.

Harassment, discrimination, bullying, retaliation, exploitation, intimidation, fraud, bribery, unauthorised clinical practice and misuse of user information are prohibited.

Personnel must not use their role to form inappropriate relationships with users, solicit money or gifts, market unrelated services, or communicate outside approved channels except where authorised and documented.

11. Partnerships, referrals and communications

Partnerships with hospitals, clinicians, NGOs, schools, employers, funders and technology vendors must be approved and documented before being represented publicly as active.

Agreements must define roles, services, eligibility, fees, referral and consent processes, data-sharing responsibilities, safeguarding, complaints, liability, service levels, termination and continuity.

Referrals must be appropriate to the user's needs and the receiving provider's verified capacity. A referral does not guarantee acceptance or a particular outcome.

Marketing must not exploit fear, shame or distress. Do not make misleading claims about outcomes, credentials, availability, partnerships or AI capabilities. Testimonials and personal stories require valid permission and appropriate privacy safeguards.

12. Complaints, incidents and enforcement

Users, staff and partners must have an accessible route to raise safety, privacy, service-quality or conduct concerns. Reports must be acknowledged, assessed, documented and handled impartially within a reasonable timeframe.

Good-faith reporting is protected. Retaliation or intimidation is prohibited. Serious safeguarding, clinical safety, security or privacy incidents must be escalated promptly and corrective actions recorded.

Breaches may result in access suspension, retraining, disciplinary action, contract termination or referral to a professional regulator or law-enforcement authority, as appropriate and lawful.

13. Review, approval and required details

Review this policy at least annually and whenever services, law, technology, risks or the operating model materially change.
